# Simulated accessibility scenarios

These three profiles are **SIMULATED**, created by agent reviewers inspecting the prototype. They are not participants, patient interviews, clinical findings, headset observations, or measured readability improvements. Their advice is a design hypothesis to test with people later. The prototype keeps automatic passthrough startup and only short instructions plus Accept regions in the headset.

| Hypothetical profile | Advice from the simulated review | Implemented response | Automated checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Central vision loss | Undo accepted mistakes; make the new copy discoverable; offer immediate original view; explain blocked movement; allow moving a backed instruction panel. | B first discards a drawing or retained failed trace; otherwise it removes newest pending, then newest accepted. Accept selects the newest copy, with a thicker contrasting outline, brief direction cue and haptic pulse. Hold left X hides effects; release rechecks fresh coverage. Left stick moves instructions and Accept together. Rejected movement keeps its last valid position and an actionable instruction until movement succeeds. | Retained-trace discard before pending/accepted undo; selected copy and paired resource cleanup; grip rollback; original-view gesture cancellation and fresh coverage after readiness/coverage loss. |
| Restricted peripheral field | A fixed head-relative panel cannot be brought into central view by turning the head. Prefer compact movable controls and a center-out copy default. Source grip must bring an unseen copy to the pointer. Explain long-stroke cancellation; mark the loop start. | The smaller, lower backed HUD fits actual rendered instruction bounds within 0.60 x 0.08 m and Accept glyph bounds within 0.24 x 0.045 m; instructions and Accept remain movable together within bounds, including camera permission/error states. Scale1 placement ranks bounded candidates nearest center and prefers clearance for enlargement through 1.25x; this is a convenience default, not a disease preset. Source grip uses zero offset; copy grip keeps its offset. The start ring uses the actual adaptive instance radius and thickens with a brief pulse only after a genuine excursion and return produce valid geometry. Failed release retains a visible trace; trigger resumes it and B discards it before undoing regions. A 513th distinct sample cancels with a restart instruction. | Measured finite/nonzero HUD bounds, lower default centers, font-refresh refit and Accept hit priority; center-first/headroom placement; source/copy grip offsets; minimum 3 distinct valid vertices with strict area/finite geometry, genuine excursion-and-return through the adaptive instance ring, retained-trace resume/discard and open-L/small-loop regressions; 512/513 sample boundary and fresh-trigger restart. |
| Low contrast and monocular aiming | Silent shrinking can make a copy harder to read. Thin colored strokes and unbacked white help can disappear on clutter. Mark pointer contact and hover; acknowledge rejection. | Acceptance keeps copies at scale1, preferring bounded clearance through 1.25x enlargement while retaining a valid scale1 fallback; if no valid placement is found, every pending region remains unchanged with a retrace/undo instruction. Explicit right-stick resizing remains available; a rejected resize does not silently move or shrink the copy. The smaller, lower instructions use white text on an opaque black backplate, fitted to measured rendered bounds rather than a clinical readability target. Region strokes use dark outer/light inner layers, with extra width for selection/hover. Contrasting endpoint/start rings mark the controller hit and loop start. Rejection pulses are rate-limited; interruption stops vibration. | Bounded enlargement clearance and scale1 fallback; crowded-canvas atomic rejection; dense valid convex/concave placement; dense 480-point R.22 circle corner-fit regression; selected stroke width; font material/queue ownership; rejection interval and pulse timeout/stop. |

Central-loss and restricted-field preferences can conflict, as can larger content and limited field of view. Manual HUD position, grip movement and explicit resizing provide user control without assuming one layout fits a diagnosis. No preset diagnoses, eye tracking, AI processing, TTS, audio dependency, or medical claim was added.

The source/copy separation check remains exact within a finite 32,768 collision-work budget. Initial placement uses bounded center-ranked scale1 and additional interior/adjacent enlargement-clearance candidates; valid scale1 fallback remains available when preferred 1.25x clearance cannot be established. Tight packing may be conservatively rejected even if a more exhaustive search could find a fit; neither packing nor enlargement headroom is guaranteed. Acceptance does not silently resize a copy, and a rejected movement or resize keeps its last valid pose. The adaptive closing radius is min(global maximum, max(0.005 m, 0.25 x maximum radial excursion)); the global maximum remains 0.03 of the canvas diagonal, rather than a fixed closure target.

Pure NUnit tests check state and geometry. Native Unity EditMode tests exercise actual meshes, materials, hit testing and resource disposal; run results are recorded separately by implementation/integration workers. Neither kind establishes human accessibility, haptic feel, optical contrast, tracking comfort, passthrough registration, or usefulness on a physical Quest 3S. Those require device and user assessment.

Primary sources informed the hypothetical profiles, rather than validating this application: [NEI low vision](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/low-vision) distinguishes central/peripheral loss and discusses larger text/contrast; [RNIB AMD](https://www.rnib.org.uk/your-eyes/eye-conditions-az/age-related-macular-degeneration-amd/) describes central detail loss; [RNIB Building Sight](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/Building_Sight_2023_lGasFEr.pdf) discusses limited peripheral vision and contrast in environments; [W3C contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) and [use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) support contrast and non-color cues as general design principles, not a measured headset compliance claim; [RNIB monocular vision](https://www.rnib.org.uk/your-eyes/eye-conditions-az/monocular-vision-sight-in-one-eye/) describes possible depth and side-field effects.
