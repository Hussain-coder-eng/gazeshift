# Simulated accessibility scenarios

These three profiles are **SIMULATED**, created by agent reviewers inspecting the prototype. They are not participants, patient interviews, clinical findings, headset observations, or measured readability improvements. Their advice is a design hypothesis to test with people later. The prototype keeps automatic passthrough startup and only short instructions plus Accept regions in the headset.

| Hypothetical profile | Advice from the simulated review | Implemented response | Automated checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Central vision loss | Undo accepted mistakes; make the new copy discoverable; offer immediate original view; explain blocked movement; allow moving a backed instruction panel. | B removes newest pending, otherwise newest accepted. Accept selects the newest copy, with a thicker contrasting outline, brief direction cue and haptic pulse. Hold left X hides effects; release rechecks fresh coverage. Left stick moves instructions and Accept together. Rejected movement keeps its last valid position and an actionable instruction until movement succeeds. | Pending/accepted undo order; selected copy and paired resource cleanup; grip rollback; original-view gesture cancellation and fresh coverage after readiness/coverage loss. |
| Restricted peripheral field | A fixed head-relative panel cannot be brought into central view by turning the head. Prefer compact movable controls and a center-out copy default. Source grip must bring an unseen copy to the pointer. Explain long-stroke cancellation; mark the loop start. | Moderate text size on a compact panel can move toward center, either side, up or down within bounds, including camera permission/error states. Scale1 placement ranks bounded candidates nearest center; this is a convenience default, not a disease preset. Source grip uses zero offset; copy grip keeps its offset. A start ring thickens at the retained closure threshold; a brief pulse cues it. A 513th distinct sample cancels with a restart instruction. | Moved HUD bounds and Accept hit priority; center-first placement; source/copy grip offsets; exact 20-sample and 0.03-diagonal closure thresholds; 512/513 sample boundary and fresh-trigger restart. |
| Low contrast and monocular aiming | Silent shrinking can make a copy harder to read. Thin colored strokes and unbacked white help can disappear on clutter. Mark pointer contact and hover; acknowledge rejection. | Acceptance preserves scale1 or leaves every pending region unchanged with a retrace/undo instruction. Explicit right-stick resizing remains available. Instructions use white larger text on an opaque black backplate. Region strokes use dark outer/light inner layers, with extra width for selection/hover. Contrasting endpoint/start rings mark the controller hit and loop start. Rejection pulses are rate-limited; interruption stops vibration. | Crowded-canvas atomic rejection; dense valid convex/concave placement; dense 480-point R.22 circle corner-fit regression; selected stroke width; font material/queue ownership; rejection interval and pulse timeout/stop. |

Central-loss and restricted-field preferences can conflict, as can larger content and limited field of view. Manual HUD position, grip movement and explicit resizing provide user control without assuming one layout fits a diagnosis. No preset diagnoses, eye tracking, AI processing, TTS, audio dependency, or medical claim was added.

The source/copy separation check remains exact with a finite collision-work budget. Initial placement tries at most13 constructive candidates per pending region and may conservatively reject tight packing that a more exhaustive search could find. No copy is silently shrunk to compensate.

Pure NUnit tests check state and geometry. Native Unity EditMode tests exercise actual meshes, materials, hit testing and resource disposal; run results are recorded separately by implementation/integration workers. Neither kind establishes human accessibility, haptic feel, optical contrast, tracking comfort, passthrough registration, or usefulness on a physical Quest 3S. Those require device and user assessment.

Primary sources informed the hypothetical profiles, rather than validating this application: [NEI low vision](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/low-vision) distinguishes central/peripheral loss and discusses larger text/contrast; [RNIB AMD](https://www.rnib.org.uk/your-eyes/eye-conditions-az/age-related-macular-degeneration-amd/) describes central detail loss; [RNIB Building Sight](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/Building_Sight_2023_lGasFEr.pdf) discusses limited peripheral vision and contrast in environments; [W3C contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) and [use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) support contrast and non-color cues as general design principles, not a measured headset compliance claim; [RNIB monocular vision](https://www.rnib.org.uk/your-eyes/eye-conditions-az/monocular-vision-sight-in-one-eye/) describes possible depth and side-field effects.
